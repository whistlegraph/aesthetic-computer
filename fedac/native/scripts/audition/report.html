Sampled Instrument Audition

Open-licensed acoustic sample libraries, each rendered the way the `1 grand piano plays: nearest anchor, pitch-shifted, linear interpolation, a 70 ms damper. Each one plays a C-major arpeggio and then a held chord. Raw note plays one recorded sample untouched.

What the audition turned up

Octave names disagree

Much of VCSL and VSCO 2 CE names middle C “C3”, so those files sound an octave above their names. This was measured with YIN pitch detection and spectral peaks. Steinway, harp, Meatbass, solo violin and Iowa follow C4 = middle C. Corrections are recorded per library as shift.

Iowa files start late

Iowa MIS samples leave room tone before each attack. Trimming everything below −50 dB is what makes the phrase land in time. Iowa's prep step needs the same trim.

Struck and plucked fit today

Our engine plays every sample through to its end. Mallets, harps, kalimba, harpsichord and pianos work as they are. Bowed and blown instruments only hold as long as their recording, so they need loop points first.

The organ reads both ways

One organ anchor measures at its labelled pitch and another an octave up, probably a strong 4′ rank. Check it by ear before choosing a shift.
