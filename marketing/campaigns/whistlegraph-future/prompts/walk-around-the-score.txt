Diegetic light only — never overlay glow, never neon, never lens flare. Candid tone — NOT cinematic, NOT an advertisement hero pose. The figure never looks at the camera. the aerial line: the score in this picture is one single continuous ribbon — a flat tapering band, banded across its width in short segments of solid color like a striped paper streamer, faceted into flat planes rather than smoothly tubular, with two or three loose thin scribble threads trailing off it. it holds its shape in the air with nothing supporting it. the colors are hot pink, marigold orange, lemon yellow, grass green, and cobalt.

the scene: one large finished whistlegraph hangs alone in white space — no room, no floor, no pedestal, no vitrine, nothing holding it. it is a BUTTERFLY: two round lobed wings either side of a narrow upright body with a small blunt head, exactly the shape in the reference photographs — but built out of the striped ribbon instead of drawn on a surface, and TURNED IN SPACE so we read it obliquely, three-quarters and foreshortened, one wing swinging toward us and larger, the far wing compressed and small. it is not flat-on. it is not symmetrical from this angle.

the butterfly fills most of the picture and is complete inside the frame, with white margin around it.

one person stands well below and behind it, seen from BEHIND — small, a sixth of the picture height, hands down at their sides, head tipped back, walking slowly around it and looking up. they are not drawing. they have already stopped drawing. a soft hatched shadow sits under their shoes and under nothing else.

attached to the ribbon itself, right where the line begins at the butterfly's lower edge, is one small hand-lettered card — a plain pale rectangle tilted a few degrees, tied to the band with a short thread, like a museum label with no wall to hang on. it reads: @JEFFREY. this tag is the ONLY lettering permitted in the picture.
