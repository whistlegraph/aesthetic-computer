A warm, hand-drawn illustration for a music-piece poster — painterly and poster-like, not a
photo. Soft off-white paper ground, confident inked outlines, gentle gouache-like shading, a
little hand-drawn texture and grain, subtle drop shadows so the machines feel like objects
resting on the page.

Scene: an overhead, top-down view of THREE small thin laptops, open, lying side by side in
one straight row across the frame, all three parallel and facing the same way, lid hinges at
the top. Keyboards and trackpads fill the frame. Redraw the machine from the laptop
reference images — same proportions, same wedge body, same flat keyboard layout, same
trackpad shape and position. Even spacing between the three, a little breathing room at the
left and right edges. Read as a block of three-flavour Neapolitan ice cream: three solid
bands of colour side by side.

The three bodies, left to right. Match the Apple product photographs supplied as
references — these are the real machine's three colours and the anodised aluminium is
matte, soft and slightly desaturated, never candy or plastic:
• LEFT — INDIGO: a dark slate navy blue, deep and a little greyed, not a bright royal blue.
• CENTRE — CITRUS: a bright lemon yellow, warm and gold-leaning. It is YELLOW, not green
  and not chartreuse.
• RIGHT — BLUSH: a soft dusty pink, warm, pale and clearly desaturated — a barely-there
  pink, not a saturated rose or magenta.

Each machine's keycaps are colour-matched to its body in a LIGHTER shade, as on the real
product: pale, near-white caps faintly tinted toward that body colour.

=== 1. THE COMPUTER KEYBOARD, COLOURED LIKE A PIANO ===

CRITICAL: each machine keeps its ORDINARY QWERTY COMPUTER KEYBOARD — the full flat grid of
small square keycaps in straight even rows, exactly as in the laptop reference photos:
function row, number row, QWERTYUIOP, ASDFGHJKL, ZXCVBNM, modifiers and a spacebar, each cap
carrying its own printed legend.

DO NOT draw a piano keyboard. DO NOT draw long white piano keys or narrow black piano keys on
the laptop body. There is no musical keyboard instrument anywhere in this picture. The
machine is a laptop and its keyboard is a computer keyboard.

The piano idea is expressed ONLY through how the existing square keycaps are COLOURED. Most
keycaps are the "white" naturals: pale, near-white, only faintly tinted with that machine's
colourway and clearly LIGHTER than its body.

The home row must be drawn with its real full complement, ending
A S D F G H J K L ; ' before the return key — the apostrophe/quote key MUST be present.

On each machine EXACTLY TWELVE of those square keycaps stand in for the "black" semitone keys
of a piano — same square keycap shape as every other cap, just filled in the accent colour.
They are, and only they are:
  • top letter row — Q, W, R, T, Y, U, O, P  (eight)
  • home row — S, and the apostrophe/quote ' key  (two)
  • bottom row — Z and V  (two)
Paint these twelve in that machine's saturated contrasting accent hue, darker and richer than
the pale naturals, and give them a soft gentle glow in their own colour:
  • the INDIGO machine's twelve semitone keys are CITRUS YELLOW
  • the CITRUS machine's twelve semitone keys are BLUE-VIOLET
  • the BLUSH machine's twelve semitone keys are DEEP TEAL

These must NOT be accent-coloured and must stay plain pale naturals: the E and I keys on the
top row — I especially, it is a natural and is a common mistake; A, D, F, G, H, J, K, L and
the semicolon ; key on the home row; X, C, B, N, M on the bottom row; and every number,
symbol, modifier and function key. Identical twelve-key pattern on all three machines.

Every key stays legible: EVERY keycap on all three keyboards clearly shows its own legend —
the letters A–Z, the number row, the symbols, the esc / tab / shift / command / option
labels, and the function-row F1–F12 icons. Do not leave any keycap blank.

=== 2. THE TRACKPAD IS A DRUM ===

Each trackpad carries the TrackDrum instrument map, copied from the square TrackDrum icon
reference image. Draw it as a set of NESTED ROUNDED RECTANGLES filling the trackpad, one
inside the next, from the outside in:
  • outermost ring — soft sage green (the hi-hat)
  • next ring in — terracotta orange, filled with fine evenly spaced DIAGONAL hatch lines
    (the snare wires)
  • next ring in — warm ochre gold (the tom)
  • innermost solid centre — deep dark umber brown (the kick)
Each zone slightly raised off the one beneath with a soft inner edge and a thin shadow, like
stacked drum pads. Keep every zone strictly inside the trackpad rectangle.

Nothing sits on top of the zones. NO touch dots, NO white spots, NO tether or connecting
line, NO fingerprints or strike marks anywhere on the trackpad — the drum surface is drawn
bare, exactly as in the TrackDrum icon reference. The keep-out applies to all three machines.

=== 3. THE SCREENS SHOW THE REAL MENU BAND INTERFACE ===

Do not invent an interface. Redraw the Menu Band interface from the supplied screenshot

correctly for the top-down view. On every screen include, stacked in this order:
  • along the very top edge, a menu bar holding a small multi-segment keyboard strip with a
    few segments lit in that machine's accent colour, and to its right a soft unreadable
    suggestion of a clock
  • a dark rounded panel showing a musical STAFF of horizontal lines with three or four small
    round coloured note-dots sitting on it, each with a gentle halo
  • a row of four small pale rounded chord chips beneath the staff
  • a grid of two rows of small square keycaps — the layout picker — with a few caps dark and

  • three stacked rounded rows beneath, the topmost one filled dark as the selected mode
Keep it small, calm and legible as shapes. Render all of it as drawn marks, NOT as legible
words, letters or numerals.

=== 4. THEY ARE TALKING TO EACH OTHER ===

Small musical notes hop in short dotted arcs from the left machine to the centre machine and
from the centre machine to the right machine, and one long dotted arc curves back underneath
from the right machine to the left, closing the circle — a single tune being passed along,
one note at a time. Draw the notes small, sparse and elegant, each arc tinted the accent
colour of the machine it left.

Clean, uncluttered, elegant, with generous negative space above and below the row for a
headline. No text, no words, no readable letters or numerals anywhere, no logos, no brand
marks, no fruit symbol on any lid, no hands, no people.
