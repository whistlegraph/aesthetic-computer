# Aesthetic Wing

## A bounded personal street-aviation platform

### Executive summary

Aesthetic Wing is a proposed personal electric vehicle combining the efficient
ground movement of a self-balancing one-wheel board with short-duration,
computer-stabilized hover.

The rider moves through lean and foot pressure. On ordinary surfaces, one central
electric wheel carries the vehicle. When the surface changes, a guarded
distributed-lift structure progressively unloads the wheel, enters a low hover,
and returns automatically to the ground.

The central Phase I research question is:

> Can a compact one-wheel vehicle transition safely between rolling, partial
> lift, and controlled low-altitude hover while preserving an intuitive
> board-riding experience?

Phase I develops the system model, propulsion test article, transition controls,
safety envelope, and an unmanned demonstrator. It does not include human-carrying
flight.

## Why now

Personal electric boards create a direct relationship between bodily movement
and transportation, but remain constrained by curbs, broken surfaces, gaps, and
steep terrain. Existing personal-flight demonstrations pursue unrestricted
flight and therefore accept high continuous power, concentrated downwash, noise,
limited endurance, and a difficult safety case.

Aesthetic Wing explores a smaller objective: **bounded street aviation**. Its
primary mobility remains an efficient wheel. Lift exists for short, intentional
intervals close to the surface.

## Technical innovation

1. **Dual-mode locomotion** — one wheel handles normal travel; lift operates only
   for skim and hover events.
2. **Deployable distributed lift** — two hinged lift petals open from a compact
   purple monowheel spine, increasing effective lift area while keeping every
   rotor guarded.
3. **Rider-intent controls** — lean and foot-pressure sensing become desired
   three-dimensional motion while stabilization remains automatic.
4. **Bounded autonomy** — software constrains height, speed, tilt, surface
   clearance, energy reserve, and landing behavior.

## Operating modes

- **Roll:** the central wheel supports the vehicle.
- **Skim:** lift partially unloads the wheel over rough ground.
- **Air step:** a short controlled rise, translation, and landing.
- **Hover:** fully airborne movement inside a deliberately low envelope.
- **Safe settle:** automatic descent after lost input, low reserve, or fault.

## Phase I objectives

### 1. Establish feasibility

Model mass, lift area, power, thermal behavior, downwash, acoustic output,
battery requirements, and wheel-to-hover transition dynamics.

**Success:** identify an architecture with credible mass and power margins for
the bounded demonstration envelope.

### 2. Validate the lift architecture

Build a stationary full-load rig and measure thrust, input power, noise,
outwash, temperature, control response, guard losses, and motor-out behavior.

### 3. Develop transition controls

Create a simulator and hardware controller for roll, partial lift, hover, and
automatic settling.

**Success:** repeatable transitions without violating defined attitude,
acceleration, wheel-load, or energy limits.

### 4. Demonstrate integrated movement

Build an unmanned weighted prototype that rolls, unloads its wheel, crosses a
small controlled interruption, lands, and resumes wheeled movement.

## Safety strategy

Phase I uses no rider. Testing begins on a stationary load rig and advances
through tethered and remotely supervised trials inside a controlled facility.
The system includes physical rotor containment, independent shutdown, battery
isolation, thermal monitoring, height limitation, automatic descent, and formal
hazard reviews at each test gate.

Public streets, flight over people, transportation claims, and unrestricted
altitude are out of scope.

## Commercial wedge

The initial market is controlled recreation and research rather than commuting:

- closed-course air-skate parks;
- resorts and destination experiences;
- performance and demonstration venues;
- personal-mobility research platforms; and
- licensing of transition-control and deployable-lift technology.

## Funding request

Aesthetic Wing seeks **$300,000 over 12 months** to establish technical
feasibility and demonstrate an unmanned wheel-to-hover transition.

The non-confidential funding thesis is a deployable distributed-lift and
transition-control system for bounded personal mobility. The product and
cultural identity remain Aesthetic Wing.
