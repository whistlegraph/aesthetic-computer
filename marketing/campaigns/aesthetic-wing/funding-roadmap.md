# Aesthetic Wing funding roadmap

Updated July 23, 2026. Verify every deadline again before submission.

## The fundable core

Do not lead a technical application with “a flying skateboard.” Lead with the
high-risk research package underneath it:

> A guarded, deployable distributed-lift architecture and transition controller
> that moves a compact vehicle between wheel-supported travel, partial wheel
> unloading, bounded hover, and automatic wheel-first settling.

The Aesthetic Wing identity and reel make the idea memorable. The research case
is thrust-per-watt under guard losses, mass and hinge stiffness, transition
dynamics, motor-out response, downwash/noise, energy reserve, and safe settling.

## Recommended funding stack

### Stage 0 — $15k–$40k: make the question credible

- prior-art and freedom-to-operate scan;
- first mass/power/downwash/noise model;
- multibody transition simulation;
- concept hazard analysis;
- letters of commitment from an aeronautics controls lead and a test facility;
- ten to fifteen customer-discovery interviews with controlled-course operators,
  resorts, research labs, and performance venues.

Use patron support, an art/technology commission, founder funds, or a small
sponsor for this stage. Do not spend it on a rider-carrying prototype.

### Stage 1 — about $300k: unmanned feasibility

Submit the current Phase I proposal as four measurable work packages:

1. architecture and energy model;
2. guarded stationary lift rig;
3. hardware-in-the-loop transition controller;
4. tethered, unmanned weighted wheel-to-hover-to-wheel demonstration.

Release funds against safety gates. The eight-foot marketing image is a design
vision; the funded Phase I test envelope should start at inches and expand only
after evidence.

### Stage 2 — $1m–$2m: integrated demonstrator

Pursue Phase II, strategic aviation partners, or a priced seed round only after
Phase I produces measured thrust, efficiency, noise, thermal, stability, and
fault-response data. This stage can add a purpose-built facility demonstrator,
manufacturing study, and certification/regulatory strategy.

## Immediate application package

1. Form the eligible US small-business applicant and begin SAM.gov/UEI and
   Research.gov registrations early.
2. Recruit named aerodynamics/propulsion, controls, battery, and test-safety
   leads; the current largest weakness is team evidence, not visual storytelling.
3. Turn `proposal.md` into the four NSF Project Pitch fields.
4. Replace broad market claims with interview evidence and a narrow first buyer:
   a controlled research or recreation operator.
5. Attach the product sheet and reel as communication aids, clearly labeled
   speculative—not prototype evidence.
6. Keep human flight, public streets, certification claims, and production sales
   outside Phase I.

## Go / no-go evidence before a full raise

- modeled mass and power budget closes with at least a stated engineering margin;
- guards, petals, hinges, and central wheel fit the same physical volume;
- downwash and noise targets are explicit;
- credible behavior exists for motor loss, sensor loss, low reserve, and jammed
  deployment;
- at least one qualified technical lead is willing to own each safety-critical
  discipline;
- at least three potential controlled-site buyers agree the bounded capability
  solves a real problem worth paying to test.
