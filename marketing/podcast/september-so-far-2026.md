Nine days into September and the dailies went quiet, so this is the log I owe you. It was a dense stretch. Not one announcement, more like a house where every room had the lights on at once.

The month opened with a television station. Whistlegraph TV had been a browser pretending to be a broadcast, and on the first I tore the browser out and let F-F-M-peg play the tapes straight to the stream, twenty-four hours a day. Then a second station for the clock room, and a bridge. When someone types in the YouTube chat, their words walk into the actual room on aesthetic computer and appear as a guest. The people watching the clock on television are, for a minute, in the same room as the people setting it.

The same day a record came out, wannadash, and a few days later the next song took over the week. I'm a butterfly. I built a thing called the hitbaker, which reads a chart of how real songs are shaped and lays an arrangement bed to match it. A cold open. A choir of butterflies stacked at one address. A pitch gate that measures every sung note against its target and iterates until it hits.

On the third, a paper went live. Taking turf. It's about slop, and the prompt corpus, and a game boy garden, and the corpus stopped being a proposal while I was writing it. Every prompt anyone types into the system is now recorded, and the misses are first class. The third section had to be rewritten the same day, because the thing it described had just started existing.

Then the fighting game. Oskiewar went through ten versions in five days, ninety-one up to one hundred, and the number is the story. The front door became a versus game. You share the link, and whoever opens it is your opponent. Strangers meet at the door. Hidden windows keep hosting. I built a botting harness and a frame tape method so I could watch two machines fight without being at either of them. It's a real multiplayer game now, which it was not on the first of the month.

The last two days were about the room itself. Notepat, the little keyboard on the native machine, learned to send its colors out through a serial port into a DMX lane, so a note played on the keys lights a real fixture on the wall. Then scores. A file format called the N-P score, which carries melody and light together, where velocity breathes the room and drums strike it white. And on the eighth, notespatial. Special Sign stepped out of the recording and into a field. Real ears, measured head-related transfer functions, so things can be pinned above and below you. The bed under my voice right now is one of those scores.

That's where the month is. The dailies should be back. If they aren't, this is what you missed.
