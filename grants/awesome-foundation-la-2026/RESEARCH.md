## The facts (confirmed live, 2026-09-24)

- **What:** "$1,000, no strings attached" microgrants. Each chapter's ten
  trustees put in $100 a month. "All chapters provide grants with no
  strings attached and claim no ownership over the projects they support."
  Not a loan, no reporting.
- **Who:** "Anyone is eligible for a grant — individuals, groups, and
  organizations alike."
- **Deadline:** **rolling; no fixed date.** The LA chapter: "We review
  applications (at least) every other month ... We generally only review
  applications from the current month's pool," and "If we didn't pick your
  project in a given month, feel free to reapply." So an application filed
  in September is in the September pool only. **File by Sept 30 to be in
  this month's pool**, and refile monthly if not picked.
- **Chapter:** Los Angeles, CA. la@awesomefoundation.org. Chapter page:
  https://www.awesomefoundation.org/en/chapters/los-angeles. Trustees
  include Scot Armstrong, Jessica Bendinger, Lizzy Caplan, Ron Livingston,
  Matt Walsh (comedy/TV people). Tone of the page: "No idea's too stupid,
  strange, sincere, or ridiculous. ... we do it over beers."
- **What LA funds (last two years):** Otay Mesa Detention Center Collective
  (May 2026), Eternal Canvas (Apr 2026), Buddy Walk, Mothers Supporting
  Mothers, foster-youth program, Copper Siren, Boyle Heights Garden Network,
  beginner cycling series, Black Men Blooming. Community-facing, concrete,
  LA-rooted. Older winners include a P-22 coloring book, Process-Based Art
  Kit, Spaces for Spontaneous Singing, Silver Lake Chorus.
- **What they don't fund:** "ones that are personal and limited in their
  community impact, and existing efforts that would not see significant
  incremental benefit from a small grant."
- **Multiple chapters allowed** if you disclose it. There are also thematic
  chapters (Conservation and Climate, Disability, On the Water) but none
  fit.

## Fit

Not an art grant; a **community-impact microgrant with a sense of humor.**
The pitch has to be a bounded, visible LA thing $1,000 finishes. Three
candidates, strongest first:

1. **A KidLisp workshop kit for an LA library or school:** six refurbished
   low-end laptops running AC Native OS, a printed KidLisp card deck, and
   two Saturday sessions where kids write tiny generative programs and
   print them. $1,000 = laptops (~$80 each used) + printing + snacks.
   Concrete, photographable, "process-based art kit" precedent.
2. **Public device library:** the SSRC framing (`ssrc-just-tech-2026/`)
   shrunk to one shelf: loaner AC laptops at a community space.
3. **Laer Klokken bell walk / free public performance** with the six-laptop
   fleet from CultureHub (`culturehub-la-2026/`), one afternoon, one park.

Lead with 1. Reuse the SSRC "digital community commons" language, cut to
2,000 characters, and make the images the CultureHub photos plus a KidLisp
print.
