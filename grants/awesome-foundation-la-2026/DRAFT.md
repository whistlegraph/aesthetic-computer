## Tell us about your awesome project! (≤ 2,000 chars) — 1189
Kids in LA get told to "learn to code" and then handed a login screen. KidLisp is the opposite: a language so small its whole vocabulary fits on one card, where (ink red) (line) draws a red line the instant you type it. It runs inside Aesthetic Computer, a free creative operating system I have been building in Los Angeles since 2021, and it boots on the cheapest laptop you can find.

KidLisp Saturdays is two afternoons at the Cudahy Library in Southeast LA. I bring six refurbished laptops running the system, a deck of printed KidLisp cards, and a photo printer. Kids (8 and up, no experience) copy a card, change one number, and watch a picture happen. By the end of the session each kid has written a real program and walks out with a print of it, signed, with the code on the back. The laptops stay with the library on a loaner shelf so the programs can keep growing after the workshop ends.

Everything is free software and stays free. Every program a kid writes gets a short web address they can type into any phone to show their family. This month the same system ran a six-laptop performance at CultureHub LA; this is the same instrument, handed to the people it was built for.

## Tell us a little about yourself (≤ 500 chars) — 345
I'm Jeffrey, an artist and programmer in Los Angeles. I make Aesthetic Computer, a small free operating system for drawing, sound, and play that runs on old laptops and phones, and I have taught creative coding at CalArts and UCLA. I'd rather kids meet computers as instruments than as products. This month: artist in residence at CultureHub LA.
