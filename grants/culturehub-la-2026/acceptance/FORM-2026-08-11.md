## 2. Short bio — third person, 50–100 words

> Jeffrey Alan Scudder is a Los Angeles–based artist, educator, and technologist
> who builds musical instruments and creative tools. He created Aesthetic
> Computer, Whistlegraph, AC Native — a bootable operating system that turns
> aging laptops into instruments — and Menu Band, a piano that lives in the
> macOS menu bar. Scudder holds an MFA from Yale and is an Author in Residence
> at UCLA Social Software. He hosts NELA Computer Club in Chinatown, Los
> Angeles. His work is held in the collections of KADIST and SMK, the National
> Gallery of Denmark.

## 3. Artist statement — first person, 50–100 words, printed as a quotation

**Use this one.** 82 words.

> At CultureHub I'm presenting two new pieces, one for AC OS and one for macOS.
> *Note(s)pat(ial)* spreads notepat across six surplus grade-school laptops, each
> booted into AC OS, the operating system I make; from a live control surface I
> turn the whole sound field through the room. *The MacNeoPolitan Trio* is newer
> and still taking shape — three MacBook Neos, each played through Menu Band, my
> menu-bar piano. One runs on machines pulled back from e-waste, the other on
> machines bought new.

Three things are doing work here. The two-platform framing (AC OS / macOS) is the
cleanest one-line explanation of why there are two pieces at all. *Note(s)pat(ial)*
still carries *notepat* inside it, so the programme reads as an expansion of the
accepted *Notepat Jam* rather than a replacement. And "surplus grade-school
laptops" is a sharper ecological claim than "salvaged" — it names where the
machines came from.
