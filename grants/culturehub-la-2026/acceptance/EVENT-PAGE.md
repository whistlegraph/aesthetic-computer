# Whistlegraph presents: *Special Sign* & *MacNeoPolitan*

Nine laptops, two works, one instrument — played from inside the score.

Jeffrey Alan Scudder presents a two-work program for laptop ensemble. *Special
Sign* expands his composition for twelve moving sound bodies onto six salvaged
laptops revived with AC Native, distributed across CultureHub LA's surround
system; Scudder rotates the whole field, changes its speed and trajectory, and
moves individual voices between machines while the audience sits inside the
score. *MacNeoPolitan* is a new spatial audio play for three MacBook Neos —
indigo, citrus, and blush — each played through Menu Band, the menu-bar piano
Scudder builds and gives away.

The two works are the same instrument on opposite ends of a computer's life: one
on machines pulled back from e-waste, one on machines bought new. A conversation
follows.

## The works

**Special Sign — live spatial version.** Six salvaged laptops, booted into AC
Native, running notepat. Expanded live from a fixed 1:41 recording into a longer
form that can change each time it is played.

**MacNeoPolitan.** Three MacBook Neos, three colors, three voices — Neapolitan,
in a block. Each machine plays Menu Band, where the keyboard sits in the menu
bar beside the clock, the WiFi, and the battery.

## Audience interaction

The audience sits inside the surround field during the concert and joins a Q&A
afterward.

A separate open session, the **Menu Band Jam**, invites participants to play
along on their own computer. Bring a Mac and install Menu Band — it is free and
open source, and takes about a minute. Bring any other laptop and boot AC Native
from a USB stick, which changes nothing on the computer's disk. No programming
or musical experience is required, and loaner machines are available.
