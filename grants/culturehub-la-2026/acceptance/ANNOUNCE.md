### Long — the full post

> nine laptops. two works. one instrument.
>
> i'm closing my culturehub la residency with a program under the whistlegraph
> banner.
>
> *special sign* started as a fixed recording — 1:41, twelve moving sound bodies.
> at culturehub it becomes six salvaged laptops booted into ac native, one
> distributed instrument inside the room's surround system. i rotate the whole
> field, change its speed, move voices between machines. it can go differently
> every time it's played.
>
> *macneopolitan* is new. three macbook neos — indigo, citrus, blush — each
> playing menu band, the menu-bar piano i build and give away. three flavors in a
> block. a spatial audio play.
>
> one work runs on computers people threw away. one runs on computers bought new
> off the shelf. it's the same instrument either way, and that's the point. a
> computer should come with something to play, the way it comes with a clock.
>
> the audience sits inside the score. come sit in it.

## Notes

- Bill the program **Whistlegraph presents** — it is the imprint tying the two
  works together and it matches the graphic-score lineage already documented in
  `papers/arxiv-whistlegraph/`.
- Say **"menu-bar piano"** on first mention of Menu Band; it does the explaining.
- Menu Band is free and open source at `prompt.ac/menuband`; the Mac App Store
  build is $4.99 and is a reduced, sandboxed subset. For workshop copy, point at
  the free version.
- Do not name the blush MacBook Neo's exact finish until the machine is bought.
- The e-waste framing is load-bearing for *Special Sign* and deliberately
  inverted by *MacNeoPolitan*. Keep both halves; the contrast is the pitch.
