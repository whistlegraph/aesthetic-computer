# Chamber study 3: process form and instrument-like envelopes

For *Note(s)pat(ial) Native*, six laptops in a ring, 13:08, 112 BPM, CultureHub LA, September 24 2026. Grid: beat 0.536 s, cycle (2 beats) 1.07 s, chord round (C Am F G, 8 beats each) 16 cycles = 17.1 s, two rounds = 34 s.

## Part A: how process ensembles build over 10 to 15 minutes

Reich, *Music for 18 Musicians*: eleven chords stated at the start, two breaths each, restated at the end. Each chord floors a four to five minute section. The mallet pulse never stops; harmony changes once per section; wind and voice chords swell at breath length. The climax is the densest interlock; the ending returns to the opening cycle.

*Six Pianos*: one 8-beat pattern, four players, two more entering two beats out of phase. Parts are built by substituting notes for rests one at a time, and thinned by the reverse. Three sections on the same seven pitch classes (D major, E Dorian, B minor) with sudden changes of centre. *Electric Counterpoint*: an 8-voice canon assembled one entry at a time; the finale adds two basses suddenly.

Riley, *In C*: 53 patterns, each repeated 45 to 90 s, players within two or three patterns of one another, an eighth-note pulse on high Cs throughout.

Adams, *Phrygian Gates*: 14 subsections, each changing one parameter (figuration, register, pulse speed, amplitude); gates are abrupt Lydian to Phrygian switches, and Lydian time shortens. *Shaker Loops*: four movements of looped tremolo; density and register carry the form.

Andriessen, *Hoketus*: two identical groups at the stage extremities, never sounding together, alternating near-identical chords; the melody exists only as the sum of two places.

Eastman: each section contains all preceding sections; nothing is removed, only buried. Pärt, *Cantus*: three bell tolls, then a descending A minor scale in prolation canon, each entry an octave lower at half speed, five octaves over the piece, with a pivot at the golden mean.

Wolfe and Lang: overlapping polyrhythms and abrupt gear shifts, not fades.

Dance arrangement: something small changes every 8 bars, bigger every 16, sections every 32; a breakdown removes kick and bass entirely; a riser or roll fills the last 2 to 4 bars before the drop.

Common thread: one lane changes one parameter per phrase; the climax is prepared by subtraction; endings restate the opening.

## Part B: envelopes from sines with linear attack and decay

Risset's bell (Dodge and Jerse) uses 11 partials at ratios 0.56, 0.56 (+1 Hz), 0.92, 0.92 (+1.7 Hz), 1.19, 1.7, 2.0, 2.74, 3.0, 3.76, 4.07, gains 1, 0.67, 1, 1.8, 2.67, 1.67, 1.46, 1.33, 1.33, 1, 1.33, duration ratios 1, 0.9, 0.65, 0.55, 0.325, 0.35, 0.25, 0.2, 0.15, 0.1, 0.075. The transferable rule: each partial has its own decay and higher partials die sooner. Karplus-Strong plucks obey the same law, so an additive pluck is harmonics 1 to 4 with decays roughly halving per partial. Marimba bars are tuned 1:3.92:9.24, xylophone 1:3:6.16; marimba decay runs from 2 s at low C to 20 ms at C7; vibraphone rings 30 s low to 6 s high. Perceived attack: under 10 ms reads as struck or plucked, 50 to 100 ms bowed, 150 to 400 ms breathed, over 1 s a pad. A synth kick is a sine falling from 150 to 48 Hz, 1 ms attack, 300 to 450 ms decay; Risset's drum adds narrow-band noise.

## Recommendations

1. Replace the single 34 s switch with three tiers: one lane changes one parameter every round (17.1 s), a lane joins or leaves every two rounds (34 s), sections change every four rounds (68 s). Reject two changes in one round.
2. Ring (1:44): build the swing by substitution. Round 1 sounds its first note only, round 2 adds the second, round 3 the third, round 4 the bass.
3. Echoes (3:09): first two rounds as a hocket. Odd seats sound the walk on beats 1 and 3, even seats on 2 and 4, never together. Full overlap returns in round 3.
4. Echoes: hold each chord 16 beats for the middle two rounds, then return to 8 in Climb.
5. Climb (5:09): each new octave enters as a half-speed canon of the walk while the previous octave persists two rounds at 0.5 gain, so registers overlap and nothing is cut.
6. Breakdown before the Lift: from 7:10 to 7:27 remove walk, taps and bass, keep pads and high line, add a white-noise riser at the back seat with 17.1 s attack and 0 decay. Double the hat subdivision in the last two cycles.
7. Land D, kick, theme and the walk's octave drop together on beat 1 of 7:27. Cap the octave-lifted peak at 8 cycles, drop the theme an octave for one round, restate at full for the final round.
8. Return (9:44): each departing lane exits with a one-round (17.1 s) linear decay starting on beat 1 of C. Order: echoes, hats, bass, pads, taps, answer. The walk stays last.
9. Bring the five long front tones from Appear back at 11:27 under the last walked notes.
10. Pads: attack 4 cycles, decay 4 cycles, staggered two cycles per seat so the swell orbits the ring, replacing the fixed 60 s revolve.
11. Apply the recipes: walk and echoes plucked, theme and answer bell, pads pad, taps and kick drum. Per-machine voices at the Lift peak: walk 4 + echo 4 + kick or snare 2 + pad share 3 + theme 5 + answer 5 + hat 1 + high line 4 = 28. Assert 28 or fewer per machine.
12. Vanish: three final front tones with silences of 2, 3.2 and 5.2 cycles, and the closing low C with 3.5 s attack and 0.5 s decay.
