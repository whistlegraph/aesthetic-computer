# Chamber study 01: timing and arrangement in continuous tonal works, 10 to 16 minutes

Reference timeline (13:07): Appear 0:00, Ring 1:44, Echoes 3:09, Climb 5:09, Lift 7:26, Return 9:43, Vanish 12:00. At 112 BPM a 4/4 bar is 2.14 s, 8 bars 17.1 s, 16 bars 34.3 s. The switch every 34 seconds is a 16-bar grid with nothing inside it.

## Reference works

Bach, Chaconne in D minor, about 15 minutes. 64 four-bar variations over one harmonic cycle: 33 in D minor (0 to 52%), 19 in D major (52 to 81%), 12 in D minor (81 to 100%). Sections shrink toward the end, so intensity per minute rises. The local peak is the arpeggio block at variations 23 to 30 (36 to 47%), followed by the major-mode clearing where rhythm and harmony simplify. Busier rhythm always pairs with simpler harmony. Variation 63 restates variation 1 before the final cadence. Every four bars cadences; all joins are elisions on the downbeat.

Bach, Passacaglia in C minor, 12 to 14 minutes. Eight-bar ground, theme plus 20 variations, then a double fugue without pause. One texture layer is added per variation; the ground migrates between bass, tenor and soprano; the widest register arrives only in the fugue.

Pärt, Fratres, 9 to 11 minutes. Nine rotations of a six-bar theme in bars of 7/4, 9/4 and 11/4, each separated by a two-bar 6/4 percussion tolling over an A-E drone. Each rotation starts in a different octave. The theme leaves a pitch centre one step further for three bars and mirrors back for three, so every phrase is an arch that closes. Dynamics peak in the central rotation (about 50%); the piece ends on the quiet tolling bars alone.

Pärt, Spiegel im Spiegel, about 10 minutes, F major. Piano triads at constant harmonic rhythm; the violin phrase starts with two notes and gains one per phrase, each ascent answered by a descending mirror returning to A. No climax: the form is the lengthening phrase, and the ending is the longest phrase closing on A.

Reich, Electric Counterpoint, 14:46, fast (6:51), slow, fast, without pause. Each movement builds a canon one voice at a time, then the soloist plays resultant patterns, then strummed chords enter. The slow movement halves tempo and changes key. The finale alternates E minor and C minor, 3/2 and 12/8, at an accelerating rate until the basses fade and it resolves in E minor.

Reich, Music for Pieces of Wood, 10 to 12 minutes. Three sections with shrinking cycles, 6/4, 4/4, 3/4. Each builds a pattern by substituting beats for rests, one note at a time, then thins. About 58 pattern shifts in ten minutes, each changing one note, so the ear hears growth, not switching. The end is abrupt and together.

Adams, Shaker Loops, about 26 minutes, four continuous movements starting 0:00, 8:51, 15:25, 22:45. The stillest music is the second movement, ending near 58%; the climax is a unison push-pull rhythm late in the third, near 80%, then a four-minute coda thins the same pulse.

Adams, Christian Zeal and Activity, 8 to 10 minutes. One hymn phrase per minute; voices displaced so chords blur. Roughly one chord change every 8 to 15 seconds.

Eastman, Stay On It, about 25 minutes. One riff; sections repeat ad lib on cue, freedom widens as the piece devolves, and each breakdown makes the riff's return an arrival.

## Recommendations

1. Phrase grid. Replace the fixed 16-bar step with lengths from {8, 12, 16, 24} bars; no length repeats more than twice in a row.

2. Phrase shape. In Echoes, Climb and Return every melodic phrase leaves the tonic by n scale steps and mirrors back, n incrementing by one per phrase and resetting per section. The return note is held one full bar.

3. Cadence rule. Every fourth phrase ends with all sounding voices on a tonic-triad tone, envelopes decaying over at least 2 bars, then 1 bar with no attack. New voices may enter only here.

4. Harmonic rhythm. Appear and Vanish: one chord per 8 bars. Ring and Echoes: per 4. Climb: per 2, then per bar in its last 16 bars. Lift: per bar. Return: per 2, 4, then 8. When chord rate doubles, halve rhythmic activity, and vice versa.

5. Climax placement. Loudest bar at 9:20 plus or minus 10 s (71%), reached by an unbroken crescendo from 7:50, not at Lift's downbeat. Return's first 30 s is the second quietest music in the piece.

6. Greatest stillness at 4:40 to 5:09 (36%): two seats only, one chord held 8 bars, no attacks, noise off.

7. Joins. Ring to Echoes and Climb to Lift: elision, last 4 bars of A overlap first 4 of B in seats 1 and 4. Echoes to Climb and Return to Vanish: 1 bar silence, then a 2-beat solo pickup in seat 1. Lift to Return: 2-bar general pause (4.3 s) after the peak, then the Appear sine alone.

8. Climb. Nine 16-bar phrases (2:17), each starting a third higher. Add a voice only every second phrase, never in a phrase where register moves.

9. Call and response. Pair opposite seats (1-4, 2-5, 3-6). Seat n states a phrase; seat n+3 answers after 2 beats with its inversion. In Echoes rotate the caller 1, 4, 2, 5, 3, 6.

10. Fills. Kick and snare enter in Lift only after a 1-bar noise fill whose 16th-note density grows by rest substitution across beats 3 and 4. Then a 2-beat fill every 8 bars and a 1-bar fill every 32, deterministic.

11. Prepare the lift. From 8:30 alternate C and D with durations 8, 8, 4, 4, 2, 2, 1, 1 bars, landing on D at 9:20; hold D to 10:50, then reverse the series to resettle on C so Vanish is in the home key.

12. Ending. Vanish restates Appear's sine at half density; each phrase doubles decay (2, 4, 8 bars). Final C held 8 bars at fixed level, released by all six on one cue, no fade. If a fade is wanted instead, fade low seats first and end on the highest sine. Not both.
