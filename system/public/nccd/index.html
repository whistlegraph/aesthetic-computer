A Mac app for the NELA Computer Club. It shows the club's Signal circles, the roles in each circle, and the club wiki, in one window. The circles are peers on one ring, so the home screen is a donut. The d is for donut.

How it works

  1. Install the app

    Open the disk image and move NCCd into Applications. Apple has notarized the app, so it opens with no warning.

  2. Install the Signal engine

    NCCd speaks to Signal through signal-cli. Install it with Homebrew: brew install signal-cli

  3. Link Signal to open your circles

    Select Link Signal. NCCd shows a QR code. On your phone, open Signal, then Settings, then Linked devices, then Link new device, and scan the code. NCCd is now a linked device of your account, as Signal Desktop is. The circles lie on the donut: a larger one has more members, and one that beats had a message a short time ago. Under the donut is the newest message of every chat: select one to go into that conversation. To give a circle a window of its own, right-click it and select Open in New Window. You see only the groups that you are a member of.

  4. Sign in to NCC to open the wiki

    The club login page shows in the Wiki tab. Sign in there. The wiki then opens in its own tab.

  5. Use one, or the two

    The two logins are separate. Signal opens the circles. NCC opens the wiki. NCCd works with one of them, and the foot of the side bar shows a tick for each one that is done.

  6. Stay up to date

    NCCd updates itself. It looks for a new version every half hour, checks it, and starts again on the same circle. You do nothing.

  7. Bring your history OPTIONAL

    A new link starts with no old messages. If this Mac also has Signal Desktop, select Import History from Signal Desktop in the menu. The import needs Node.js.

What talks to what

Security

Signal messages
NCCd is a linked device of your Signal account. signal-cli does the encryption with Signal's own library, so messages stay encrypted end to end, the same as on your phone. Nobody between your Mac and the other members can read them.
What a linked device receives
A linked device receives all messages that your account receives. NCCd keeps and shows only the messages of the club groups, and drops the others. signal-cli saves the attachments that it receives in its own folder on your Mac.
Club login
The club's own login page shows inside NCCd, in a web view. You type your password into the club's page. NCCd does not read or keep what you type. It reads one thing from that web view: the session that the wiki gives you when the club has confirmed who you are. The wiki, not NCCd, decides what you can read.
Wiki session
The session is in your login Keychain. NCCd sends it only to the club wiki, over HTTPS. Sign Out of NCC in the menu deletes it.
The app
The app is signed with a Developer ID, uses the hardened runtime, and is notarized by Apple. It is not sandboxed, because it starts signal-cli. It has no analytics and no crash reports. The source is public.
Updates
NCCd reads a small file on assets.aesthetic.computer to learn the newest version. It sends nothing about you. Before a new version replaces the app, it must pass three checks: its SHA-256 is the published one, its code signature is whole, and it is signed by the same Developer ID team as the copy that is running.
This download
The download link counts the download: the version, the day, the country and the type of computer. Your address is kept only as a keyed hash.
Limits
NCCd is a beta. signal-cli is not made or supported by Signal, and NCCd is not affiliated with Signal. The message logs on your Mac are plain files: they are as safe as your Mac account, so turn on FileVault.

Remove it

On your phone, open Signal, then Settings, then Linked devices, and remove NCCd. Move the app to the Trash. Delete the two folders in the table. Messages already sent stay in the groups.
