CLAIM

The article treats the algorithm not only as a mathematical procedure but as a
long cultural arrangement of gazes, images, prediction, and power. It moves from
al-Khwarizmi, Algol, and Medusa through Roger Caillois, Ernst Jünger, Jacques
Lacan, Walter Benjamin, and Susan Buck-Morss. Its central figure is the
mirror-shield: an image technology that protects and extends its bearer while
also arresting, capturing, and converting whoever looks into it.

The contemporary screen inherits that double action. It shows images, records
touch and attention, searches accumulated image stores, and uses those traces to
anticipate the next image. The Nvidia name and eye-shaped mark close the loop:
the mythic hostile gaze returns as commercial graphics and AI infrastructure.
The essay refuses both technological boosterism and nostalgic refusal; its
method is historical and allegorical rather than explanatory.

AESTHETIC COMPUTER

The article names the exact medium Aesthetic Computer works inside: a screen
that receives touch, renders images, stores traces, and addresses people through
handles. AC is not outside the Medusa problem because its images are playful or
handmade. Its canvas, user records, telemetry, image generation, and social
surfaces can all become forms of the reciprocal gaze the essay describes.

AC’s clearest answer is structural. It has no recommender feed or advertising
market at its front door. A person initiates a named piece through a prompt or
URL; the piece runs as an instrument in immediate mode; source can be inspected,
copied, and forked. This redirects the screen from rapt spectatorship toward
action. The contrast already appears in the Platter’s “Sucking the Complex” and
“URL Tradition” papers: a piece is reached by a name rather than selected by an
engagement-ranking surface, and the interface is meant to be used rather than
watched.

That is a design response, not an exemption. AC still has to keep collection
minimal and legible, distinguish operational telemetry from behavioral
prediction, preserve user agency at every surface, and avoid letting discovery
or generation harden into an opaque ranking system. Read this article as a test
for every AC feature: does the image help the person act, or does it capture the
person so the image system can keep acting?
