The work

The program picks a brush or a filter from a weighted deck, animates a live proposal over the painting, and waits. The person never draws a mark and the machine never decides what stays, so every painting is a negotiated history of accepted accidents.

It began in 2016 as a small iOS experiment, became a free website in 2020, and the Summer 2021 release is what still runs at nopaint.art, on phones and desktops. In 2026 the source was recovered and verified, saving was restored, and the loop was rebuilt natively inside Aesthetic Computer at notepaint.art.

The story

I wrote No Paint because I wanted to paint without the blank page and without the mouse. The program makes the first move; I only have to answer. The deck is weighted the way a studio is weighted: common stamps, a soft brush that comes up often, a wanderer that almost never does. Two buttons are the whole interface.

When the free version went out in 2020, strangers started sending stamps and asking to make brushes, and I merged each one by hand. Watching non-programmers learn how software is made by contributing to a toy they liked is what pushed me to build Aesthetic Computer, where nothing waits on me to be merged.

In 2026 I went back for the original: recovered the project file, indexed all 32,184 paintings into a searchable gallery, restored saving, and rebuilt the loop natively. A program is finished when it stops being maintained. This one is not finished.

The artist

Jeffrey Alan Scudder (b. 1989, USA) is an artist, composer and programmer in Los Angeles who builds and performs his own painting and music software. BFA Ringling College of Art and Design, MFA Yale School of Art; he has taught at Parsons, UCLA and Southern Oregon University.

No Paint was his first program to find a public of its own. Since 2021 he has built Aesthetic Computer in public: a creative computing environment with KidLisp, a language for children and artists, and AC OS, which boots salvaged laptops straight into instruments. With Alex Freundlich and Camille Klein he developed Whistlegraph; The Longest Whistlegraph Ever (so far), commissioned by Rhizome, premiered at the New Museum in 2022. His work is held by KADIST and SMK, the National Gallery of Denmark. In 2026 he was a resident at CultureHub Los Angeles and opened Everything Computer at tiat.place, San Francisco, with three singing laptops. He hosts the NELA Computer Club in Chinatown, LA.
