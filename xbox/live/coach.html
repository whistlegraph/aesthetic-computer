add your coach

your own Claude, sitting in on your fights.

Every oskiewar match has a room, and the title screen prints its name under start. A coach is an agent that takes the room's read-only seat and reads the same frames every phone in the grandstand reads: every swing, every hit, what you were doing when it landed, how often you block, how you die. It never presses a button. While it is seated the title screen says coach linked.

Two lines

The coach is one file with no dependencies. Fetch it, hand it to Claude Code as an MCP server, and Claude has the tools. 

curl -fsSLO https://oskiewar.com/coach.mjs
claude mcp add coach -- node ./coach.mjs

Then open oskiewar, read the room name under start, and tell Claude:

coach me in regga890

Codex and anything else that speaks MCP over stdio works the same way: codex mcp add coach -- node ./coach.mjs. Node 22 or newer is the only requirement.

What a coach can do

  • Watch. coach_watch streams the fight as events: hit by WHIP while airborne at distance 140, blocked KICK, died to BASH while attacking, round results. Ask it to call out what it sees between rounds.
  • Analyze. coach_analyze folds everything it has seen: hits taken by attack and by what you were doing, block rate, each move's thrown/landed/blocked/whiffed, air time, spacing. Ask what keeps getting me killed?
  • Get your data. coach_record pulls your track record from the replay ledger — rounds and matches won, opponents, every room you have played in — and coach_replay opens any stored round, match or room. Ask for your record against one handle.

Things to say to it

  • Sit in on regga890 and tell me after each round what to change.
  • Which of my moves lands least? Should I stop throwing it?
  • Pull @jeffrey's record and tell me who beats them.
  • Watch the next two minutes and count how often I jump into a whip.

What it can see, and what it cannot

The relay is public and match-scoped: anyone with a room's name can watch it, the same way anyone with the address can join the second chair. A coach reads fighter positions, states, scores and the moves on screen. It does not see your keyboard, your account, or anything outside the room. It cannot press a button, and the game counts it apart from human spectators — the antenna on the title means a machine is reading, not one more person in the stands. Up to four coaches can share one room.
