# Excerpts and poems

Checked against the linked texts on 2026-09-21. Short selections, with links
to the surrounding passages or complete poems. The notes are editorial readings.

## Tao Lin — *Taipei* (2013)

> Two months into freshman year he had committed to not speaking in almost all situations.

Silence becomes a habit.

[Published excerpt, VICE, May 28, 2013](https://www.vice.com/en/article/an-excerpt-from-tao-lins-taipei/).

## Ernest Hemingway — “Big Two-Hearted River,” Part I (1925)

> There had been this to do. Now it was done. It had been a hard trip. He was
> very tired. That was done. He had made his camp. He was settled. Nothing
> could touch him. It was a good place to camp. He was there, in the good
> place. He was in his home where he had made it. Now he was hungry.

Repetition makes finishing a task feel like shelter. Then hunger brings the
paragraph back to the body. Read the preceding tent-making passage too:
the assurance comes after the work.

[*In Our Time*, Boni & Liveright, 1925; text on Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/In_Our_Time/Chapter_14).
Public-domain passage in the United States.

## Ann Beattie — “Janus” (1985)

> She had asked her husband to please not drop his house key in it. It was
> meant to be empty.

A household request carries the attachment. The last sentence gives emptiness
a purpose without explaining the marriage.

[The New Yorker, May 27, 1985 issue](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1985/05/27/janus).
Passage in the paragraph about keeping the bowl at home.

## Tao Lin — “hamsters are heads with little characteristics on the head, part three” (2008)

> in the evening the hamster sits at the computer

An animal in an ordinary human routine. Read through the final withdrawal of
empathy: the poem's reassurance becomes uncomfortable. Useful for writing a
machine as a character without making every line announce the device.

[*Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy*, Melville House; complete poem at Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/141815/hamsters-are-heads-with-little-characteristics-on-the-head-part-three).

## Tao Lin — “thirteen of twenty-four” (2008)

> react to disappointment by being quiet and nice\
> and alone, not by being confrontational or frustrated

Advice that sounds reasonable until the line break leaves someone alone.
The surrounding absurdity matters: this is a comic, unstable speaker, not
a rule for how to behave.

[*Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy*, Melville House; complete poem at Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/141816/thirteen-of-twenty-four).

## Ernest Hemingway — “Along With Youth” (1923)

> A porcupine skin,\
> Stiff with bad tanning,\
> It must have ended somewhere.\
> Stuffed horned owl\
> Pompous\
> Yellow eyed;\
> Chuck-wills-widow on a biassed twig\
> Sooted with dust.\
> Piles of old magazines,\
> Drawers of boy’s letters\
> And the line of love\
> They must have ended somewhere.\
> Yesterday’s Tribune is gone\
> Along with youth\
> And the canoe that went to pieces on the beach\
> The year of the big storm\
> When the hotel burned down\
> At Seney, Michigan.

Complete poem. The inventory reaches loss through worn objects, then lands
on a named place. It also connects to the burned Seney of “Big Two-Hearted
River.” A useful model for a machine's history told through what remains.

[*Three Stories & Ten Poems*, Contact Publishing Co., 1923; Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59603/pg59603-images.html).
Public domain in the United States. Line breaks and the spelling “biassed”
follow this edition.

## Frank O’Hara — “A Step Away from Them”

> A glass of papaya juice\
> and back to work. My heart is in my\
> pocket, it is Poems by Pierre Reverdy.

The metaphor has a physical object inside it: a book in a pocket. Read the
whole walk for how errands, appetite, and remembered deaths share one
afternoon without becoming separate speeches.

[*Lunch Poems* (1964); complete poem at Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/42662/a-step-away-from-them).

These selections allow more than one register: long sentences, awkwardness,
humor, and named feelings belong here too. The ballad's rules in the
[manifest](manifest.md#digest) are choices for that piece, not laws these
writers always obey.
