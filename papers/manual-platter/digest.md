# Digest

Read on 2026-11-16 from contact sheets of twelve evenly spaced pages per book
(`sources/sheets/`, cut by `fetch-sources.mjs`). These are layout notes from
looking at sampled pages, not readings of the texts. The cite-only shelf was
not looked at and has no entry here.

## plancy-1863-dictionnaire-infernal

**Page.** Two justified columns under a running head that carries the first
and last entry on the page as three-letter cues (`CAB — 126 — CAB`), the way a
dictionary does. Entry names are bold and run straight into the text. A new
letter gets a large centred capital and nothing else.

**Pictures.** Wood engravings sit inside the column at column width, or break
across both columns when the subject is a scene. Each has a one-word caption.
Most spreads have no picture at all; the book is mostly words.

tools:
- Running cues at the head of a reference page let a reader land by flipping.
- A portrait belongs beside the name it illustrates, at column width.

## topsell-1658-four-footed-beasts

**Page.** One wide column with a ruled head (`Of the Goat. 189`) and a narrow
outer margin holding short side-notes that name what each passage is about.
The side-notes are the index; the body never stops for a subheading.

**Pictures.** One large woodcut opens an animal, taking a third to half of the
page, and the text flows on underneath. Insects get small cuts set in a strip
down the edge.

tools:
- Margin notes label a long passage without breaking it.
- One big picture at the start of a subject is enough.

## wells-1913-little-wars

**Page.** A single narrow column of large type with wide margins, a small-caps
running head, and almost no apparatus. Rules appear as ordinary paragraphs
under a small-caps sidehead (`Size of the Soldiers`).

**Pictures.** Two kinds. Photographs of real games get whole pages. Small
drawings printed in one colour, red, walk through the margins and across the
foot of the text: a cannon firing along the bottom edge, a soldier standing
against a ruler. The red drawings carry the humour and the photographs carry
the proof.

tools:
- One spot colour, used only for the drawings, is the whole decoration.
- Marginal figures can show a rule (a man beside a ruler) faster than a sentence.
- This is the closest ancestor to the Magician's Manual's simple diagram.

## waroffice-1896-war-game-on-a-map

**Page.** One column. Numbered rules, lettered sub-points, centred chapter
heads between short rules. Side-heads in the left margin name the paragraph
(`General idea.`, `Umpires.`, `Collisions.`). The last third of the pamphlet is
tables: marching rates, column lengths, establishments.

**Pictures.** None.

tools:
- Margin side-heads again, here for looking a rule up under pressure.
- Put the tables at the back, together, where a hand can find them.

## oss-1944-simple-sabotage

**Page.** Typewritten, one column, numbered to three levels: `5.`, `(b)`,
`(1)`. Section names are underlined italics. No pictures, no tables. Every
item is one instruction. The declassification stamps top and bottom came later.

tools:
- When every line is an action, numbering is all the structure needed.
- The spell tables in the Magician's Manual work the same way: one spell, one line.

## wotc-2023-srd-5-1

**Page.** Two columns, sans-serif throughout, with three sizes of red heading
doing the navigation. Tables have a bold header row and banded rows. A monster
is a stat block: name, an italic size-and-type line, then labelled lines and a
six-column row of ability scores between rules. No art at all.

tools:
- The stat block is a fixed shape, so a reader learns where to look once.
- Colour is spent on headings only.

## gonnerman-bfrpg-core-rules-r97

**Page.** Two columns in one serif face, a running head naming the game on one
side and the chapter on the other. Spells and monsters are run-in bold names
followed by a short two-column table of fixed fields, then prose. Tables sit
in the column, ruled top and bottom.

**Pictures.** Black line drawings set into the column, wrapped by text, about
one per spread in the monster chapter and fewer elsewhere.

tools:
- Name, small table, paragraph, drawing: the monster entry at its plainest.
- A chapter name in the running head is cheap orientation.

## bfrpg-field-guide-r22

**Page.** The same entry shape as the core rules, repeated for eighty pages.
Entries are alphabetical and the running head just says `MONSTERS`.

**Pictures.** Nearly one per entry, by many hands, in styles that do not match.
The mismatch reads as a community, the same way AC's pieces do.

tools:
- A fixed entry shape lets many contributors add pages without an editor.

## osric-2-2

**Page.** Two columns, sans-serif, with a small centred chapter name at the
head and the first and last entries of the page in its top corners, the
dictionary cue again. Tables are tinted green, which is the only colour inside
the book. Monster and spell entries open with a stacked list of labelled
fields.

**Pictures.** Occasional black drawings, some taking most of a column.

tools:
- Corner cues plus a tint for tables: two devices, both for finding things.

## What the shelf says about the Magician's Manual

1. Its spell tables are the right unit. Every book here that teaches rules
   settles on a short fixed row.
2. It has no finding aids, and at two pages it needs none. A longer edition
   wants corner cues or margin side-heads before it wants more pictures.
3. One spot colour for drawings (Wells) and the interface's own colours for the
   screen (the current title diagram) are the same move.
4. The books with a voice (Wells, Collin de Plancy) are the ones written by one
   person. The committee books are easier to look things up in.
