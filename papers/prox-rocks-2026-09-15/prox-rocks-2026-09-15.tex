{\ttfamily\fontsize{7.6pt}{9pt}\selectfont\color{acpink}\textls[80]{HOW A ROCK GETS ITS NAME}\par}\vspace{0.3em}
{\fontsize{7.9pt}{9.6pt}\selectfont\color{acgray}\RaggedRight
The pet name is drawn from the session id alone: an FNV-1a hash seeds a SplitMix64 stream that picks three to six letters, consonant then vowel, so the same session is always the same word on every machine. A Loopboy keeps the name on its contact route instead, which is why surizu became surizo when it was adopted at 16:01. An Easel rock is named after the piece it holds.\par}
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.315\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}
{\ttfamily\fontsize{7.6pt}{9pt}\selectfont\color{acpink}\textls[80]{HOW A ROCK GETS ITS SHAPE}\par}\vspace{0.3em}
{\fontsize{7.9pt}{9.6pt}\selectfont\color{acgray}\RaggedRight
The shape and strata come from a second seed, the session id joined to the current seven-word summary of the latest prompt, so a rock shifts a little with every prompt while the name holds still. The nine rocks still on the ledger are drawn from their live seed. The eighteen that have gone were re-seeded from the last prompt each one answered, the state they were in when they went dark.\par}
\end{minipage}\hfill
