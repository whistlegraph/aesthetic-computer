# lines-platter

Reading platter for lines, making, and grown marks: how lines carry time, how
artifacts stay unfinished, and how programs and drawings can be farmed rather
than authored. Feeds [`neofarm/`](../../neofarm/), [`cancelok/`](../../cancelok/),
the Piecefarm lineage
([`experiments/intelligent-terrarium/`](../../experiments/intelligent-terrarium/)),
and the whistlegraph line practice
([`whistlegraph-platter/`](../whistlegraph-platter/)).

Index and citation only. `sources/` is gitignored: it holds the lecture
transcript and private correspondence materials. **The Sage Jenson materials
are from private correspondence and must not be published or quoted publicly
without Sage's permission.**

## Digest

**Ingold, "Thinking through Making" (lecture).** Against hylomorphism — the
model where a form held in the mind is projected onto shapeless matter, where
theory leads and practice applies, and where creativity is read *backwards*
from finished object to novel idea. Ingold reads creativity *forwards*:
joining the flows of materials and the movement of imagination as they feel
their way ahead in real time. Improvisation, not innovation ("a new baby is
not an innovation"). Nothing is ever finished; every artifact is a way
station. The artifact is a **knot** — an open structure whose strands trail
off into other knots — and a surface is not a container but a zone of
interchange. Materials are known by what they *do* under the hand (the maker
as alchemist, finding and bending the grain), so making yields **knowledge
from the inside**, against science's knowing-from-outside. His sustainability
is simply "being able to carry on." Against joined-up **networks** (nodes,
fully connected, no room left for life) and for the **meshwork**: everything
a line, no line with a beginning or end, loose ends everywhere to pick up and
take further. Closing image: the maker's skill is holding the imagination's
forward reach in tension with the drag of materials — "eyes trained on the
far horizon while still engaged in the labors of proximity." Blackboard over
PowerPoint, because the blackboard line binds movement, awareness, and
material in one performance while the projector merely projects.

**Captured: the blackboard/PowerPoint passage (07:28–08:28).** Clip:
`sources/ingold-blackboard-clip.mp4` (+ `.wav`). Verbatim: "I don't believe
in PowerPoint, because when we use PowerPoint we project images on a screen.
PowerPoint is the epitome of the logic of projection, which I'm arguing
against. And the reason why I like blackboards is that the blackboard is the
epitome of the process of creativity that I'm arguing for. When you stand at
the blackboard and you scrape a line, then your movement, your awareness, the
trace of the materials are all bound up in that one performance — and what
you see is the outcome of that performance. That's why I like blackboards and
don't like PowerPoint."

tools: `neofarm/` is this digest operationalized — organisms as knots
(lineage strands trailing into parents), improvisation-forwards (mechanical
mutation with no finished state, only way stations), materials-are-what-they-
do (the ISA's semantics are its practical behavior under the gate), and the
meshwork over the network (per-host lineage shards tangling through git, not
a master graph). The farm's honest gap against Ingold: a novelty archive is
still an outside-knower's instrument; the cancel/ok verdict loop and
production attention metadata are the correspondence channel.

**Sage's "Accretion Summer" notes.** Images contain time — computational,
perceptual, evolutionary, geological — at multiple scales; the summer's
question is how much *legible* time one image can hold. A drawn line contains
more time than a photograph (Hockney); painting stores and speculates on time
(Joselit); every camera-roll image contains "full gesture archeology."
Van Eyck's many-drawings method as "an antidote to the claustrophobia of 3D
space"; drawings that get smaller with no loss of detail, done with
particles. Emergence as creative process — many entry points, coevolving,
locking together. The system↔scribe diagram names "the tension and synergy
between points and fields." Painting, this summer, is "compressing the
perceived space of the world into thin overlapping layers"; a gaussian splat
may be a painting, albeit an algorithmic one. The PhysPain render — the notes
eaten by the substrate into membrane tissue, past legibility — is the thesis
enacted: writing becoming pure accreted time.

tools: the pair (legible ↔ digested) is a two-point scale for `neofarm/`'s
missing legibility descriptor — the gate currently measures coherence but
not *how much readable structure* survives an organism's own dynamics.
