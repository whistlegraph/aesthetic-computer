# 02 · Wub bass: wobble, growl, neuro, riddim, and the screwed 808

Shelf: wub, mid, 808 · Sources: `sources.json`

Companion to `01-dub-bass.md` (the floor). This entry is the character layer above the sub: the resonant LFO wobble and why it dates, what replaced it (FM growls, formant and wavetable movement, notch and comb motion, resampled neuro passes, riddim's hard gated squares), the sub/mid law, distortion staging, LFO rates that work at 128–140 half-time, mono and phone translation, and the witch-house and cloud-rap 808 practice the `climbalift` track borrows. Consumers: `pop/dance/synths/wobble.mjs` (Reese through a resonant low-pass), `skrill.mjs` (FM plus two swept formants), and the sub/808 bed under `pop/notespatial/`.

## Unit

Beat divisions as LFO rates. At 140 BPM: bar 1.714 s (0.583 Hz), half note 857 ms (1.167 Hz), quarter 428.6 ms (2.333 Hz), dotted eighth 321.4 ms (3.111 Hz), quarter-triplet 285.7 ms (3.5 Hz), eighth 214.3 ms (4.667 Hz), eighth-triplet 142.9 ms (7.0 Hz), sixteenth 107.1 ms (9.333 Hz); two bars 0.292 Hz, four bars 0.146 Hz. At 128 BPM: bar 1.875 s (0.533 Hz), half 937.5 ms (1.067 Hz), quarter 468.75 ms (2.133 Hz), dotted eighth 351.6 ms (2.844 Hz), eighth 234.4 ms (4.267 Hz), eighth-triplet 156.3 ms (6.4 Hz), sixteenth 117.2 ms (8.533 Hz). A sidechain release equal to one quarter is 60000/BPM ms (`connaghan-2021-emastered-sidechain`). Serum's LFO runs 0–20 Hz free or snaps to note values down to 1/32 when synced, and in Trig mode restarts on every note (`xfer-serum-manual`).

Register: sub C1–C2, 32.7–65.4 Hz, midi 24–36; 808 roots C1–G1, 32–49 Hz (`audeobox-2026-fl-808s`). The growl band that FM packs with partials is 100–400 Hz (`skrill.mjs` header, restating the same practice). Vowel formants, adult male averages: /i/ F1 240 Hz F2 2400; /e/ 390, 2300; /a/ 850, 1610; /ɑ/ 750, 940; /ɔ/ 500, 700; /o/ 360, 640; /u/ 250, 595 (`wikipedia-formant`). Phones pass little under 200 Hz and nothing under 100; the phone band for a bass note is 250–700 Hz (`lavoie-2026-landr-phone-bass`).

## Facts

**Reese and the wobble.** The Reese bass is Kevin Saunderson's 1988 "Just Want Another Chance" line, made on a Casio CZ-1000 with phase-distortion synthesis; it ran through jungle and drum and bass (Renegade "Terrorist" 1994, Alex Reece "Pulp Friction" 1995, DJ Trace "Sonar" 1998) into dubstep (`wikipedia-reese-bass`). Phase distortion (Casio, 1984) bends the read-out of a carrier with piecewise-linear modulators locked to the cycle, giving flatter spectra than FM and a fake resonance made from a windowed sine at the resonant frequency (`wikipedia-phase-distortion`). The wobble is "simply a bassline that has an LFO assigned to a Low Pass Filter"; start from anything but a sine; 1/8 is the starting rate; sine LFO for smooth, square for hard open/close, saw for fast attack and slow release, triangle for a ramped sine; the sub under it is a 40–50 Hz sine whose LFO, if any, goes to amplitude (`rendr-2008-dubstepforum-wobble`). A 2010 wobble layer: 24 dB low-pass in series, LFO 2 to filter cutoff at a synced 1/8, mono, 570 ms release, a sine added at the fundamental, soft distortion, a parallel distortion chain, and variants that modulate a band-pass in parallel or both filters at once (`smith-2010-sos-dubstep-secrets`). Sync the LFO to tempo; a sine LFO "offers a smoother movement than saw or square" (`jenkins-2010-sos-dubstep-basics`). Caspa (Reason only) is "always into LFOs and basslines", Rusko (Acid) into drums and chords; both cut dubplates "to sound bassy, loud, and crispy" (`host-2008-xlr8r-caspa-rusko`). Rusko's "Cockney Thug" (2009) and "Woo Boost" (2010) and the FabricLive.37 mix (2007) are the wobble's mainstream moment (`wikipedia-rusko`). Wobble arrived "in a later period" than the DMZ sound, first as "subtle modulated bassline-wobble" (Skream, Silkie) that kept atmosphere; Rusko is "generally credited with popularizing 'new' dubstep" with harsher tones, and Skrillex's labelling of his own work as dubstep fixed "loud, lurching and aggressive" as the public idea of the genre (`crossfadr-2013-war-of-wubs`). The wobble reads as dated because it is one gesture: a single resonant peak swept by one fixed-rate LFO, so every note has the same contour; a critic quoted in the same article calls vocal brostep bass "the autotune of this decade" (`crossfadr-2013-war-of-wubs`). Producers moved to modulating the spectrum (FM index, wavetable position, formant) instead of one filter, and to slower, bar-locked modulation (below).

**Growls, formants, wavetables.** "Scary Monsters and Nice Sprites" (22 Oct 2010, 140 BPM, B♭) began as "FM8 Test" after Noisia's Nik showed Skrillex Native Instruments FM8 (`wikipedia-scary-monsters`); a widely-copied recipe for that growl is FM8 in Ableton through Ohmicide distortion, two auto-filters, a WOW filter, an overdrive, EQ and a limiter (`larsson-2014-loopmasters-skrillex-fm8`). A formant is a vocal-tract resonance; two moving band-pass peaks at F1/F2 make any tone read as a vowel, which is what the talk-box does with a real mouth (`wikipedia-formant`). Massive's oscillators have a Wt-Position knob that "scans through all of the waveforms included in the wavetable" for morphing, and an Intensity knob whose Formant mode "controls the additional transposition of waveform formants", producing "morphing vowels" when modulated; Spectrum mode acts like a low-pass on the harmonics; three Bend modes warp the read-out (`ni-massive-manual-oscillator`). In Serum: FM depth around 30% on OSC A from OSC B pitched an octave up for metallic partials; LFO to wavetable position at 50% depth (tearout) or 20–30% (yoy); riddim automates the wavetable position "on every other bar"; growl LFOs at 1/2 note for slow evolution and 1/8 for rhythm; notch filters alongside MG low-pass 12/18/24; resonance 40–50% for the riddim squelch; a riddim transient pitch envelope of +12 semitones falling to 0 in about 30 ms; amp envelopes with 0 ms attack and 300–400 ms decay (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`).

**Neuro.** Neurofunk grew out of techstep in the mid-to-late 1990s (Reynolds coined it in The Wire, December 1997; Ed Rush & Optical's Wormhole, 1998); its basses come from wavetable and FM synthesis with moving mid harmonics over a constant low weight; Noisia, Phace and Mefjus are the reference producers for heavy processing and resampling (`wikipedia-neurofunk`). Noisia formed in Groningen in 2003 (Roos, van Sonderen, de Vlieger), Split the Atom 2010, Outer Edges 2016, final show 2022 (`wikipedia-noisia`). A neuro patch in Massive: two wavetables, one two octaves down with Bend intensity above 50%, 33% noise, a band-reject filter with cutoff and bandwidth near 25%, glide 66%, pitch bend ±24, an LFO synced to 1/1 (once per bar) moving wavetable position 33% in opposite directions on the two oscillators and filter cutoff 50%, tube distortion at 60% wet 90% drive, a frequency shifter, a high-shelf EQ wobbled by a 1/16 sine LFO, mono legato (`attack-2015-twisted-neuro-bass`). A Noisia-style Operator patch at 174 BPM: FM from operator B at +17 semitones, 65 ms attack, pitched down 38 semitones by a MIDI pitch device; the chain is Erosion (noise 10 kHz) → EQ (mids +6, highs −7 above 4.5 kHz) → short reverb (425 ms, 12% wet, 35% stereo) → OTT at 45% → Saturator 12% wet; layers of a xylophone and a percussion sample each get their own EQ, drive and OTT (100% on the xylophone); the bus is Saturator +10 dB → OTT 50% → limiter (`attack-2023-noisia-operator`). OTT is Xfer's free three-band upward-and-downward compressor, born as an Ableton Multiband Dynamics preset, with Depth (wet), Time (all attack/release), In and Out gain (`thornton-2024-production-expert-ott`). Resampling is the method: print the pass, cut the good bar, reload, process again; Virtual Riot records two-hour knob-twisting sessions, chops them, and reloads them as wavetables and sampler material (`antares-virtual-riot-interview`).

**Riddim.** A dubstep subgenre from Croydon, early 2010s, mainstream around 2015; "repetitive and minimalist sub-bass and triplet percussion arrangements" at 140–150 BPM; Jakes is often called the first, Subfiltronik "established what riddim is known as today"; modern riddim uses LFO square waves, wide delays and flanger/chorus filters (`wikipedia-riddim`). Infekt credits Subfiltronik, DJ Shiverz and Coffi's 2012–13 Dubstep FM broadcasts as "blueprint riddim"; the genre's marks are space, reverb, atmosphere, "super dark textures", repetition and flow rather than escalation; 140 BPM, "can work at 150 but it's a different vibe" (`infekt-ukf-guide-to-riddim`). Riddim recipes: hard clip at 40–50% drive then a compressor; wavetable position change every other bar; the gate is the pattern, not the LFO (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`, `bassgorilla-riddim`).

**Sub separate from mid.** When layering, cut the mid layer below 80–100 Hz (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`); high-pass secondary bass layers around 100 Hz and keep the sub phase-coherent below 100 Hz because PAs sum it (`senior-2012-sos-mixing-bass`); a sine sub cannot be low-pass wobbled, so its motion is amplitude (`rendr-2008-dubstepforum-wobble`); mono below about 100–200 Hz for vinyl and single-sub PA (`stewart-2018-flotown-center-sub`); 808 practice says mono below 120 Hz (`peak-studios-808-mixing`) or 200 Hz (`audeobox-2026-fl-808s`). Stereo belongs to the mid layer and its reverb (35% stereo on the Noisia layer, `attack-2023-noisia-operator`).

**Distortion staging.** A standard chain is EQ → distortion → OTT/multiband → EQ → limiter at −1 dB (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`); Noisia-style chains put a harsh stage first (Erosion, tube), an EQ, then OTT, then a gentle saturator, and repeat at bus level (`attack-2023-noisia-operator`). Parallel distortion beats EQ for small-speaker audibility but must be phase-checked; low-pass it at 2–3 kHz so the hiss does not blur the vocal (`senior-2012-sos-mixing-bass`). For 808s: a parallel distortion track high-cut at 150 Hz feeding a 200–400 Hz band, harmonics of 40 Hz landing at 80/120/160 (`peak-studios-808-mixing`); soft clip at −3 to −6 dB threshold, 5–15% FM for edge, low-pass the distorted layer at 500–800 Hz, high-pass at 20–25 Hz (`audeobox-2026-fl-808s`). Multiband distortion targeting 700 Hz–1.5 kHz adds harmonics for phones (`lavoie-2026-landr-phone-bass`).

**Sidechain.** Up to 6 dB of kick-triggered compression on a bass before pumping is obvious; fastest attack short of clicking; release matched to the kick's decay and the tempo (`aisher-2013-attack-basslines-sidechain`). A subtle setting is 2:1, attack ≤2 ms, release under 50 ms, 2–3 dB; for dance music "time the release to the track", quarter = 60000/BPM ms (`connaghan-2021-emastered-sidechain`). Or duck 2–3 dB per kick (`senior-2012-sos-mixing-bass`). 808 practice: kick controls the 808's compressor with "short attenuation, fast release" (`peak-studios-808-mixing`).

**Phone translation.** Phone hardware filters out below 200 Hz and loses everything under 100; put the note's energy in 250–700 Hz; layer an octave up; parallel-compress the mids; mix in mono; high-pass reverb at 200–400 Hz (`lavoie-2026-landr-phone-bass`). The ear rebuilds a missing fundamental from its harmonics; telephones drop everything under 300 Hz and speech pitch survives (`wikipedia-missing-fundamental`). Even a small speaker reproduces the overtones that saturation adds (`peak-studios-808-mixing`).

**SALEM and witch house.** Chopped and screwed is DJ Screw's Houston technique from the late 1980s: slow the record to 60–70 quarter-note BPM, pitch falling with it, plus skips, scratches and stop-time; it seeded witch house, vaporwave, cloud rap and phonk (`wikipedia-chopped-and-screwed`). Witch house (Salem, formed 2006; the name coined as a joke by Pictureplane in December 2009) is hip-hop drum machines and trap loops, high keyboard effects over "heavily layered basslines", dense reverb, vocals pitched down, reversed or whispered, screwed tempo, industrial and drone (`wikipedia-witch-house`). Salem's King Night (2010) is "chopped and screwed hip-hop with stripped-down drum machines" and an "808-fuelled" Redlights remix; distortion is "just another layer of the sound"; Jack starts with a beat and the others improvise melody over a few notes (`powers-2010-fact-salem`). John Holland: "it's a good idea to slow down any music, not just rap music" (`doran-2010-quietus-salem`).

**Cloud rap and Drain Gang 808s.** Cloud rap (term from 2010, Lil B and Clams Casino, A$AP Rocky's "Peso" 2011) is reverb-heavy, ethereal chopped samples, slow; Yung Lean's "Ginseng Strip 2002" (2013) and Drain Gang carried it to Sweden (`wikipedia-cloud-rap`). Bladee's Eversince (2016) is produced by Whitearmor, who says "everything I want to say has always been in the melodies" and gives no gear (`yalcinkaya-2022-dazed-whitearmor`); Yung Gud works in Ableton Live 9, started on psytrance at twelve, and names Lex Luger for making "this trap thing big" (`bell-2014-rbma-yung-gud`). The 808 itself: sine oscillator, low-pass and VCA, made 1980–83; its decay control lengthens the kick until, tuned, it stops being a drum and becomes a bass; Rick Rubin is credited with lengthening and pitching it into bass lines (`wikipedia-tr-808`). Current 808 practice: tune to the key with the important notes at 30–60 Hz (below about 28 Hz they vanish) (`peak-studios-808-mixing`); roots C1–G1 (32–49 Hz), attack 0 ms, decay 3–5 s, sustain 0, release 100–200 ms, notes ringing 1–2 s at 130–150 BPM, a 1–2 semitone pitch drop over 10–30 ms for the click, glide between notes, peaks at −6 to −3 dB, 2:1 to 4:1 compression (`audeobox-2026-fl-808s`).

## Rules

R1 two layers minimum, three for the phone. Sub: sine, C1–C2 (midi 24–36), mono, high-passed at 30–40 Hz, low-passed at 100 Hz, no filter movement, amplitude only (`rendr-2008-dubstepforum-wobble`, `mala-2008-rbma`, `skream-2006-rbma`). Mid: the same pitch one or two octaves up (midi 36–60), high-passed at 80–100 Hz, all character here (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`, `senior-2012-sos-mixing-bass`). Phone layer: a parallel distorted copy of the mid, band-limited 250–700 Hz, or the mid itself pushed into 700 Hz–1.5 kHz by multiband drive (`lavoie-2026-landr-phone-bass`).

R2 levels: no source gives a sub-to-mid ratio. Rule of thumb: sub 3–6 dB under the mid at the meter, phone layer 12–18 dB under the mid; if the bus is bass-heavy, the kick and sub share a band (`senior-2012-sos-mixing-bass`).

R3 mono/stereo split at 100 Hz for club, 120–200 Hz for streaming; sub and the low half of the mid mono; stereo only from chorus, reverb at ~35% width or unison detune on the mid (`stewart-2018-flotown-center-sub`, `peak-studios-808-mixing`, `attack-2023-noisia-operator`).

R4 wobble (`wobble.mjs`): 2–3 saws detuned, 24 dB low-pass, LFO on cutoff; sine LFO for breath, square for gate, saw for pluck (`rendr-2008-dubstepforum-wobble`, `smith-2010-sos-dubstep-secrets`). Rates 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 and their triplets and dotted forms (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`), retriggered per note. Keep resonance under self-oscillation unless the squeal is the point; a single fixed-rate resonant sweep is the sound that dates, so vary the rate per phrase and add a second, slower modulator (R6) (`crossfadr-2013-war-of-wubs`).

R5 growl (`skrill.mjs`): FM index swept hard so partials land in 100–400 Hz; carrier:modulator ratios near integers for pitched growl, +17 semitones or +1 octave for metallic (`attack-2023-noisia-operator`, `presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`); two band-pass formants stepping between vowel pairs from the F1/F2 table, /o/→/i/ (360/640 → 240/2400) for "yoi", /a/→/u/ (850/1610 → 250/595) for "yow"; the formant LFO runs slower than the FM LFO (`wikipedia-formant`, `ni-massive-manual-oscillator`).

R6 the modern rate law: one fast modulator on the grid (1/8 or 1/16, or an eighth-triplet) and one slow modulator locked to the phrase (1/1, 2 bars, or a half note) moving wavetable position, formant or notch (`attack-2015-twisted-neuro-bass`, `presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`). Rhythm from the note pattern and gate, not from the LFO alone: rests, sample-and-hold steps, a new wavetable position every other bar (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`, `bassgorilla-riddim`).

R7 gate shapes: riddim hit = 0 ms attack, 300–400 ms decay, pitch +12 semitones falling to 0 in 30 ms; hard clip 40–50%; the space between hits is the design; triplet placement (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`, `infekt-ukf-guide-to-riddim`, `wikipedia-riddim`). Wobble gate = the LFO's square; neuro gate = glide 66% into the next note with legato (`attack-2015-twisted-neuro-bass`).

R8 drive order: saturate the raw mid before the filter for density, distort after the filter for edge; then EQ, then OTT-type multiband (45–50% depth, 100% on a thin layer), then a soft saturator, then a limiter at −1 dB; never drive the sub (`presetdrive-2026-dubstep-serum`, `attack-2023-noisia-operator`). Parallel distortion is phase-checked and low-passed at 2–3 kHz (`senior-2012-sos-mixing-bass`).

R9 resample: render the mid layer, cut one bar, reload as a sample or wavetable, process again; two or three passes; keep the sub unresampled (`antares-virtual-riot-interview`, `wikipedia-neurofunk`).

R10 sidechain: kick → sub and mid, attack 0–2 ms, release one quarter (60000/BPM ms: 428.6 ms at 140, 468.75 at 128) or the kick's decay if shorter, 2–3 dB for a dub feel, up to 6 dB for a pump (`connaghan-2021-emastered-sidechain`, `aisher-2013-attack-basslines-sidechain`, `senior-2012-sos-mixing-bass`).

R11 the screwed 808 (climbalift): tune to key with roots C1–G1 (32–49 Hz); attack 0, decay 3–5 s, release 100–200 ms; 1–2 semitone drop over 10–30 ms; glide between adjacent notes; soft clip at −3 to −6 dB; distorted parallel layer low-passed 500–800 Hz; slow the whole part to a felt 60–70 BPM by writing at half-time, and pitch it down with the tempo (`audeobox-2026-fl-808s`, `peak-studios-808-mixing`, `wikipedia-chopped-and-screwed`, `wikipedia-tr-808`). Vocals and pads over it pitched down and reverbed; distortion as a layer, not a fix (`wikipedia-witch-house`, `powers-2010-fact-salem`).

R12 phone check: render the mid layer alone, band-pass 250–700 Hz, and confirm the note is audible; if not, add the octave-up layer or raise drive in 700 Hz–1.5 kHz; high-pass every reverb and delay return at 200–400 Hz (`lavoie-2026-landr-phone-bass`).

## Debts

The "why wobble dates" argument is one article's framing plus inference; no producer is on record saying the resonant sweep is cheesy. Skrillex's own chain is unpublished; the FM8 recipe is a tutorial's. Noisia's method is reconstructed from Attack tutorials and a Wikipedia summary, not their words; the resampling count sometimes quoted (24 passes) is forum lore and is not used. The Virtual Riot page returned 503 at fetch time; its resampling description is from a search excerpt. The Serum manual PDF was not text-extractable; its LFO figures were confirmed against a secondary tutorial summary. No source gives sub-to-mid or phone-layer level ratios in dB; R2 says so. The Peak Studios page is undated. Whitearmor and Yung Gud give no technique; the Drain Gang 808 rules are current trap-808 practice applied to their idiom, not their stated method. Formant values are adult-male averages; a growl's formants need not sit at speech values, only move between two peaks. The BassGorilla riddim tempo (130–135) contradicts Wikipedia and Infekt (140–150); Infekt's figure is used. The MusicRadar wobble tutorial is listed for context, unused.
